# The lifecycle constitution states that an authored change is complete at governance intent

Some changes deliver nothing that construction renders. A constitution, an invariant or a schema is
written by a person. So are the derive route's code and its sealed documents. A design has no family
for any of them, and the renderer has no builder.

Such a change still runs the phases from the seed to governance intent. The problem, the beliefs and
their verification, the gaps, the capabilities, the invariants and the ownership all apply. It is
complete there, at governance intent. It is not halted before design intent. The gate that reviews a
dossier as a body is presented at governance intent, and the gate that locks a mandate has nothing to
lock.

Today that ruling is written only in the transformation repository's account of a change. The
lifecycle constitution, which governs every dossier, does not state it. The first route change judged
by the phases looked for the ruling in the composition, did not find it, and relied on the
repository's statement instead.

A clause that governs dossiers belongs in the constitution that governs them. We want the lifecycle
constitution to state three things. A change whose deliverable is authored, not constructed, runs the
phases from the seed to governance intent, and none of them is optional. It is complete at governance
intent. Its review gate is presented there, and approving it remains the reviewer's act.

The design compiler refuses a design that amends an artifact it cannot author. Nothing refuses a
design intent for an authored change that only adds one, such as a new invariant. We want an
invariant, governed by the lifecycle constitution, that a change whose seed declares it authored has
no design intent. The design compiler realises it.

The constitution is authored, so this change is itself an authored change. It is complete at
governance intent.

Out of scope: the design compiler's rule and the seed's declaration of delivery, because the design
language is changed by the transformation repository; rendering a constitution from registers; and
changing which families construction renders.
