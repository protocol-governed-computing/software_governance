# Governance Surface Map

The semantic classification layer of the platform governance universe: what governance concepts exist, what each governs, where it is enforced, and which parts of the universe are covered, deferred, or out of scope.

This document is the standard-facing taxonomy. It is **not** a directory layout and it does not define identity. Identity is declared per artifact in its `## Machine` block; this map is the concept space that identity and the eventual PGC standard are written against. Machine-readable coordinates for every artifact are in `governance_surface_map.yaml` (regenerated by `protocol_compiler/scripts/gen_governance_surface_map.py`); the conceptual model is in `pgc_charter/doc/governance_semantic_classification.md`.

The map answers, for the MNP: *what governance concepts exist, which lifecycle phase each governs, what property it controls, where enforcement happens, which artifacts implement it, and which concepts are intentionally deferred or out of platform scope.*

## 1. The bridge

```
        PGC STANDARD TAXONOMY          concern · property · lifecycle · locus · authority
                   │
                   ▼
        GOVERNANCE_SURFACE_MAP         semantic concept ⇄ artifacts ⇄ declared namespace
                   │
                   ▼
        DECLARED ARTIFACT IDENTITY     fb.execution_topology::… · fb.governance::… · domain::…
                   │
                   ▼
        PHYSICAL REPOSITORY            folders · files
```

Arrows point downward for discovery; **identity flows upward from declaration**. A concept's home is its property, not its folder.

---

## 2. Coordinate schema

Every governance artifact carries one coordinate (in the YAML):

```
CONCERN
├── domain              concern family    (declaration | composition | execution_* | state | evolution | meta_governance)
├── concern             semantic subject  (topology, placement, vocabulary, …)
├── property            the specific thing governed
├── lifecycle           declare → compile → seal → execute → evolve
├── enforcement_locus   compile_composition | snapshot_declaration | runtime_execution | meta_process
├── authority           constitution | snapshot | process | domain
└── coverage.status     implemented | intentionally_deferred | not_applicable | process_enforced
```

`domain`/`concern`/`property` say **what** is governed; `lifecycle`/`enforcement_locus`/`authority` are orthogonal annotations of **when**, **where**, and **who**. The same concern may appear at multiple lifecycle gates without becoming multiple concerns.

## 3. The governance universe (concern families)

135 platform artifacts project onto six families:

| family | what it governs | artifacts |
|---|---:|---|
| **declaration** | what may be named and expressed (vocabulary, identity, structure, schema) | 19 |
| **composition** | what may coexist and connect (topology, authority, conformance, surface closure) | 77 |
| **execution_envelope** | under what conditions compiled behavior may execute | 17 |
| **execution_semantics** | what execution means and what evidence it produces | 3 |
| **evolution** | how the governed system may change | 2 |
| **meta_governance** | how governance itself is made valid | 17 |

State/entity is a seventh family in the concept space but has **no platform artifacts** — it is domain-owned (§5).

---

## 4. Enforcement-locus families

The locus is derived from real fields (`enforcement_stage`, `applies_to_kinds`, the governing constitution's `enforcement_model`), not assigned:

| locus | meaning | artifacts |
|---|---|---:|
| `compile_composition` | invariants that run against every compile | 108 |
| `meta_process` | governance-of-governance and process doctrine | 19 |
| `snapshot_declaration` | one active policy contract per sealed snapshot | 4 |
| `runtime_execution` | execution doctrine enforced at run time | 4 |

The four `snapshot_declaration` artifacts (`cryptographic_trust`, `placement`, `scheduling`, `security_domain` — each `*_DECLARED`) are one isomorphic pattern instantiated four times: *one declared policy → one snapshot-scoped contract → runtime consumption*. The map records them as instances of that shape, not four unrelated domains.

---

## 5. Coverage ledger

Completeness is a claim about the *declared* universe, evaluated per concern. Every concern resolves to exactly one status; anything else is a gap.

| coverage | count | notes |
|---|---:|---|
| `implemented` | 127 | governing artifact present and (for compile/runtime loci) executed |
| `intentionally_deferred` | 5 | all of `fb.transport` — transport phase frozen, TI/TE governance not yet authorized |
| `process_enforced` | 3 | `fb.lifecycle` + federation-boundary doctrine — real by declaration, no compiler teeth |

Two dispositions are explicit, not gaps:

- **transport** — `intentionally_deferred`. Distinct from *absent*: the concern is named and its governance is scheduled for the transport phase.
- **state / entity (platform)** — `not_applicable`. `ENTITY` governance is domain-owned; the platform does not govern domain state. This is a scope boundary, not a missing capability. Its first concrete instance arrives with the blockchain domain.

One finding previously recorded in the ledger, now resolved:

- **trace / observability** — `implemented`. `CONSTITUTION_TRACE_EXECUTION` formerly sat inside the `fb.topology` composite, a misplaced boundary (execution semantics, not composition). The namespace rename moved it to `fb.trace`, its own concern.

## 6. Semantic concept → current identity

Namespace identity is declared per artifact in its `## Machine` block, not derived from the folder.
Folders are discovery-only. The current distribution of artifacts across folders and namespaces:

```
composition/authority           → fb.authority(8)
composition/conformance         → fb.conformance(5)
composition/topology            → fb.artifact(1), fb.capability_contracts(9),
                                  fb.capability_side_effects(4), fb.capability_transforms(6),
                                  fb.execution(4), fb.execution_topology(11), fb.intent(4),
                                  fb.runtime_binding(6), fb.structure(3),
                                  fb.surface_contract(10), fb.workflow(5)
composition/event               → fb.event(1)
declaration/identity            → fb.actor(3), fb.artifact(2)
declaration/structure           → fb.structure(10)
declaration/vocabulary          → fb.vocabulary(6)
evolution                       → fb.lifecycle(2)
execution/envelope              → fb.cryptographic_trust(3), fb.execution_placement(3),
                                  fb.execution_scheduling(3), fb.security_domain(3)
execution/envelope/transport    → fb.transport(11)
execution/semantics             → fb.execution(2), fb.trace(1)
meta_governance                 → fb.artifact(3), fb.compiler(5), fb.conformance(2),
                                  fb.event(2), fb.federation(1), fb.governance(3)
```

The former `FB_*` composites are retired: `fb.constitution` (29 artifacts across three unrelated
families) and `fb.topology` (66 spanning composition and execution semantics) no longer exist. Each
artifact is now classified under its true architectural concern, which is the point of separating
the semantic layer from the identity layer.

Folder and namespace are deliberately *not* one-to-one — `composition/topology` alone holds eleven
namespaces. A folder realignment so that directory tracks namespace remains available as a later,
purely physical change; it would not alter any FQDN.

## 7. How this is used

- **For the standard.** The concern families, properties, and coverage ledger are the material from which the PGC governance taxonomy is reverse-engineered — independent of one reference implementation's folder growth.
- **For completeness.** A proposed governance addition is admitted by the concern-property test (`governance_semantic_classification.md §12`): what property does it own, why is it not already owned, what authority, what artifacts, what evidence. Lifecycle and locus are coverage annotations, never grounds for a new boundary.
- **For the recursive boundary.** Platform coverage is the substrate; a domain completes the surface for the concerns it introduces (notably `state/entity`). The blockchain migration is the first test of that recursion.

The coordinates are data, regenerated from the artifacts; this document is their reading. If they disagree, the artifacts win — regenerate.
